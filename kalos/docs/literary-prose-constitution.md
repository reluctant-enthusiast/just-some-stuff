# Kalos — Literary, Prose, and Narrative Constitution

**Status:** Binding editorial canon for the Age Eight phase and presumptive baseline for later life phases.  
**Branch:** `kalos-milestones-0-1`  
**Scope:** Prose, focalization, rhetoric, dialogue, scene architecture, player interaction, and editorial acceptance standards.

This document consolidates the approved literary and narrative direction. It supersedes any earlier implication that the project should maximize aphoristic density, expose the simulation’s psychological interpretation directly in prose, or structure scenes principally as visible decision hubs.

---

## 1. Governing objective

*Kalos* should feel like a life being inhabited, not a branching argument about character.

The simulation may track complex state explicitly: trust, resentment, appetite, fatigue, public belief, promise debt, attention, opportunity, and causal history. The prose should not routinely announce those abstractions. It renders physical events, partial perception, social action, and remembered residue. Meaning emerges across time.

The central editorial rule is:

> Render the event fully. Interpret it partially. Let later scenes complete—or revise—the meaning.

The player should sometimes read for several minutes without being asked to decide anything. Other people act without waiting for Kalos. Work, play, conversation, weather, and household life carry narrative momentum independently of the choice interface.

---

## 2. Focalization

The default mode is close third person, with optional close second person in interactive rendering.

The prose may be lexically mature, but it remains epistemically constrained by Kalos’s age, experience, location, and available evidence.

At eight, Kalos may notice with unusual accuracy:

- who controls the center of a room;
- where a laugh can land;
- when an adult is listening while pretending not to;
- whether a person’s stillness is deliberate;
- whose hands are trusted with a task;
- when a social arrangement has shifted.

He should remain capable of serious misinterpretation about:

- why Seli wants to travel;
- whether Aro likes, tests, resents, or merely observes him;
- what a diplomatic gift obligates adults to do;
- why practical restitution may not restore trust;
- whether his generosity was sincere, performed, or both;
- what adults mean when they use household and kinship shorthand.

The narration may reveal contradiction. It should rarely resolve contradiction into doctrine within the same paragraph.

### Event, belief, and meaning remain separate

1. **Objective event:** what happened.
2. **Immediate perception:** what Kalos saw, heard, and physically experienced.
3. **Provisional belief:** what Kalos currently thinks happened or means.
4. **Public account:** what others say occurred.
5. **Later meaning:** what the event comes to signify after subsequent experience.

These layers may diverge for years.

## 3. Prose hierarchy

The prose should not maintain a uniformly heightened literary register. Ordinary prose must carry most of the life so lyricism, comedy, terror, and insight have room to rise.

Working intensity budget:

- **70% transparent narrative prose**
- **20% heightened sensory or rhythmic prose**
- **10% genuinely lyrical, comic, or aphoristic prose**

This is a hierarchy, not a quota.

### Rhetorical patterns to ration

Use sparingly:

- “not X, but Y” constructions;
- paired corrective fragments;
- symmetrical moral contrasts;
- retrospective life theses;
- scene endings that state the lesson;
- sentences that provide image, interpretation, and conclusion at once.

A sharpened sentence can crown a scene. Repeated sharpened sentences flatten it.

### Interpretation lag

Important meaning should often arrive later through:

- changed conduct;
- another person’s refusal;
- an altered work assignment;
- a repeated phrase;
- an object retained or discarded;
- a later scene in which the earlier choice acquires a new cost;
- adult memory that differs from the childhood event.

### One governing material image per scene

Each major scene should have one principal material or sensory center:

- **Low Water:** mud, grounded canoes, and displaced belongings.
- **The Sweet Oil:** oil caught in the grain of a bowl and the bodily persistence of taste.
- **The Borrowed Bed:** bedding, warmth, breath, and redistributed space.
- **Words Through the Wall:** interrupted sound, cloth, darkness, and incomplete sentences.
- **The Cave at Half Tide:** reflected green water, shell markers, cold stone, and changing access.

Other details may appear, but they should not compete equally for symbolic importance.

## 4. Syntax by activity

### Household and routine

Use longer, accumulating sentences when simultaneous tasks and claims overlap. Interruptions, cross-talk, moving bodies, bowls, bedding, smoke, and work should create syntactic crowding without sacrificing clarity.

### Work

Favor procedural precision:

- hand position;
- sequence;
- resistance;
- correction;
- repetition;
- the exact point at which attention fails or skill becomes automatic.

Technical knowledge appears through conduct, not exposition.

### Play and humor

Use timing, interruption, imitation, delayed information, and audience response. Kalos’s humor must be dramatized often enough that the player understands why he is unusually funny. The prose should not merely report that everyone laughed.

### Shame

Narrow perception. Let irrelevant detail fall away. Hands, exits, witnesses, and the truth-bearing object become disproportionately salient. Avoid naming shame unless the word is necessary.

### Danger

Prioritize spatial and causal clarity over lyricism. The player must understand:

- where each body is;
- what surface moved;
- what route remains open;
- who can reach whom;
- how much time exists;
- what the attempted action physically costs.

### Wonder

Permit lyric expansion without translating wonder into a lesson. Beauty may exceed Kalos’s understanding and remain unconverted into plot.

## 5. Dialogue constitution

Dialogue performs social action. Characters conceal, press, joke, mishear, simplify, refuse, flatter, redirect, and bargain. They do not exist to state the project’s thematic interpretation.

### Kalos

- quick recombination of other people’s language;
- often improves or redirects an existing joke;
- uses partial explanations when ashamed;
- more comfortable changing the room than directly stating a need;
- occasionally startlingly candid;
- may make someone laugh without repairing the injury.

### Neri

- technical, literal, and economical;
- does not volunteer emotional explanation;
- anger makes him more exact rather than more eloquent;
- possesses dry humor that others may miss;
- refuses Kalos’s preferred framing by asking concrete questions;
- is competent but not infallible.

### Oren

- states disputed rules as remembered fact;
- speaks confidently before deciding what he thinks;
- narrates himself favorably;
- resents public correction more than private error;
- possesses real practical competence in selected domains;
- must sometimes protect, teach, or perform courage without an audience.

### Aro

Aro is not the designated outsider who immediately sees through Kalos.

She should be:

- observant but fallible;
- tired, proud, playful, abrupt, or mistaken according to circumstance;
- protective of her household’s dignity;
- occupied by responsibilities that precede Kalos;
- capable of an important misreading of him;
- independently connected to Seli, Neri, Oren, Pali, and her own household;
- stronger than Kalos in some contexts and less verbally agile in others.

Her dialogue should be concrete. Replace diagnostic lines with immediate questions or accusations a child would actually use.

### Pali

- concrete and literal;
- repeats adult phrases without preserving their meaning;
- assigns motives to animals and objects;
- capable of refusal, invention, and mischief;
- not merely a vulnerability or occasion for Kalos’s protectiveness;
- sometimes creates the best joke accidentally.

### Seli

- compressed, increasingly private, and impatient with Kalos’s assumptions;
- does not explain herself simply because he asks;
- can seek his social help without yielding control of her future;
- states actions concretely: “I asked Veya about going,” not a polished speech about autonomy.

### Adults

Adults may:

- withhold information;
- simplify inaccurately;
- manage other work while speaking;
- contradict one another;
- assume children understand customs they have never been taught;
- use family history as shorthand;
- make errors of judgment.

## 6. Scene architecture

Avoid the recurring structure:

> evocative opening → visible dilemma → four complete philosophies → short consequence → distilled lesson

Use this structure instead:

1. **Existing motion** — people are already working, eating, arguing, traveling, or playing.
2. **Intrusion** — weather, arrival, appetite, error, rumor, or bodily need changes the pattern.
3. **Attention** — the player may determine what Kalos notices; the objective scene continues.
4. **Autonomous movement** — other people act without waiting for Kalos.
5. **Hinge** — one consequential action becomes necessary.
6. **Repercussion** — the choice changes the second half of the scene through dialogue, work, space, and access.
7. **Residue** — the scene ends on an object, changed arrangement, bodily sensation, or unfinished exchange.

A major choice should not normally terminate the scene immediately.

### Prose cadence

Flexible working ranges:

| Unit | Typical prose |
|---|---:|
| Quiet or routine scene | 900–1,500 words |
| Major anchor scene | 2,000–3,500 words |
| Minor interaction response | 150–350 words |
| Major decision response before next input | 500–1,000 words |
| Scene-ending aftermath | 400–800 words |
| Seasonal interlude | 500–1,000 words |

A major branch response should ordinarily include:

1. enactment;
2. independent reactions;
3. practical repercussions;
4. an unresolved afterimage.

Quiet prevention, obedience, ordinary pleasure, and uneventful exploration receive the same prose care as visible failure.

## 7. Restrained player interaction grammar

The player influences more than conduct but does not control the entire narrative world.

### Read

Most of the experience. The world unfolds without input.

### Attend

Occasionally choose what continues drawing Kalos’s attention. This changes rendered detail, acquired information, memory salience, and visual focus—not objective events.

Example:

> The meal continues. What does Kalos keep watching?

- the oil left inside the bowl;
- Aro’s untouched spoon;
- Eda counting bowls again;
- Veya’s hand on the sealed pouch.

Use roughly once per major scene at most.

### Interpret

Rarely choose Kalos’s provisional belief when evidence is incomplete.

Example:

> What does Kalos think “one child” means?

The selected answer becomes belief, not truth, and may later prove wrong.

Use two or three times across the whole Age Eight phase, not routinely.

### Act

Choose Kalos’s conduct. Most scenes should contain one major hinge and zero to two smaller actions. Available actions should depend on prior state rather than presenting the same comprehensive strategy menu in every playthrough.

### Follow

At one formal transition per arc, the player may choose which autonomous thread the narrative witnesses while Kalos is absent or peripheral. The player does not control the selected character.

### Remember

At major transitions, the player may mark which concrete image remains most vivid. This changes memory salience, prose callbacks, dreams, and visual motifs—not history.

### Linger

Occasionally offer a scene-specific alternative to moving on:

- remain until the light leaves the cave roof;
- sit beside the fire after the bowls are cleared;
- leave while there is still room near the wall.

Lingering may produce no secret, reward, or punishment.

### Excluded interaction patterns

Do not add:

- frequent hotspots;
- inventory puzzles;
- visible relationship meters;
- dialogue wheels for every exchange;
- direct NPC control;
- constant look/listen/touch prompts;
- codex exposition replacing lived knowledge;
- reflex mechanics;
- direct emotion selection;
- unrestricted perspective switching.

## 8. Age Eight Arc II — binding revision directives

### Arc center

The visitor arc concerns **hospitality, space, and unstable belonging**.

It may also contain appetite, secrecy, authority, wonder, and risk, but those are secondary pressures. The arc should not attempt to fully explain every adult contradiction already known about Kalos.

### Required order

1. **Low Water**
2. **The Sweet Oil**
3. **The Borrowed Bed**
4. **Words Through the Wall**
5. **The Cave at Half Tide** or an equally developed tide-pool route
6. **What Stayed**

The Borrowed Bed must precede Words Through the Wall because the altered sleeping arrangement creates the physical conditions for overhearing.

### Low Water

- Keep: “The rain stopped before dawn, but the settlement went on shedding it.”
- Treat the gull as a short conditional disturbance, not a full strategy hub.
- Let prior position and attention determine the immediate gull outcome.
- Make the canoe arrival the primary hinge.
- Give Aro an urgent household task before she attends to Kalos.
- Show redistribution concretely through bedding, bowls, wet coverings, counted stores, and work.

### The Sweet Oil

- Separate Kalos’s own portion from any later opportunity involving the pouch.
- “Eat it and enjoy it” remains a legitimate, ordinary route.
- Render appetite bodily before interpreting it psychologically.
- Give Aro an independent reason for not eating her portion.
- Her reaction to theft or barter may be mistaken, approving, resentful, curious, or practical.

### The Borrowed Bed

- Begin after adults and older children have already started rearranging space.
- Kalos enters a redistribution in progress; the house does not await his solution.
- Preserve the cold-place route in which discomfort purchases no special knowledge.
- Let play and humor become group-authored rather than wholly Kalos’s achievement.
- Give Seli, Eda, Pali, and Aro’s household real influence over the final arrangement.

### Words Through the Wall

- First establish incomplete fragments.
- Then ask what Kalos thinks they mean.
- Preserve the belief as potentially false.
- Adult explanation remains bounded even if he is caught listening.
- Seli’s future continues independently of Kalos’s response.
- Use concrete dialogue: “I asked Veya about going.”

### The Cave at Half Tide

- The cave is conditional, not mandatory.
- A tide-pool route with Pali receives equal prose, beauty, and consequence.
- Aro’s reason for going must precede Kalos.
- Neri’s preparation reduces risk but does not eliminate uncertainty.
- Oren must possess at least one real competence.
- No-event cave visits should occur often.
- The worked object, if retained, has social ambiguity and no automatic quest significance.
- Preparation changes exposure; danger is not moral punishment.

### What Stayed

- Select two or three consequences, not every state callback.
- End on a concrete continuation.
- Approved tonal model:

> Above the work shed, Neri’s shells clicked against the beam whenever the wind turned. No one had yet taken them down.

Do not end with a general thesis about what life is made of.

## 9. Ensemble guardrails

Age Eight must not reduce recurring figures to stable functions.

Required reversals across the phase:

- Pali initiates, refuses rescue, or creates a plan.
- Neri makes at least one reasonable technical mistake.
- Oren performs genuine courage or competence without social reward.
- Mara misjudges a person, task, or environmental condition.
- Eda’s generosity creates work or cost for someone else.
- Seli seeks Kalos’s assistance in a way that does not surrender her autonomy.
- Aro misreads Kalos and develops at least one important relationship that excludes him.

Some scenes should center another person’s problem. Some events should have no moral lesson. Some beautiful experiences should remain beautiful and inconsequential.

## 10. Persistence standards

### Preserve exactly

- named promises;
- serious injuries;
- shared secrets;
- disputed objects;
- major public stories;
- explicit grants or breaches of authority;
- Seli’s journey status and what Kalos believes about it;
- the visitors’ storage arrangement;
- high-salience sensory memories.

### Compress into patterns

- appetite conduct;
- humor as welcome, coordination, concealment, or status capture;
- respect for domain competence;
- hospitality behavior;
- personally providing care versus transferring care safely;
- repeated disclosure, concealment, or private repair.

### Usually discard

- ordinary weather without consequence;
- minor mud, cold, and fatigue;
- incidental laughter;
- exact sleeping location after the arc;
- low-salience dialogue variation;
- the gull incident unless it materially changes relationship, resource, or public story.

### Persist autonomous world state

- whether Aro’s household receives storage;
- how much food was lost;
- Aro’s trust in East Inlet;
- Seli’s independent interest in travel;
- Eda and Veya’s disagreement;
- changes between Neri and Oren;
- Pali’s security in the crowded household.

## 11. Editorial acceptance checklist

A scene is not ready merely because it is grammatical, atmospheric, and branch-complete.

Before approval, verify:

- Does the scene have momentum before the player’s decision?
- Do other people act independently?
- Is Kalos’s interpretation distinguishable from objective truth?
- Does the prose understand less than the engine?
- Is there one dominant material image rather than several competing symbols?
- Are children’s lines concrete and age-plausible?
- Is humor actually dramatized?
- Does a major choice change lived scene time rather than only a state summary?
- Does the route preserve mixed outcomes without narratorial grading?
- Does the ending leave residue rather than declare a lesson?
- Could the same event plausibly be remembered differently by another character?
- Is there enough ordinary life that the dramatic pressure feels embedded rather than scheduled?
- Has the scene avoided making Kalos the necessary center of every problem?
- Would the quiet or preventive route remain satisfying at equal prose length?

The target is not prose that continuously advertises literary ambition. It is prose whose power feels discovered in the work, bodies, weather, humor, and incomplete understanding of the life being shown.
