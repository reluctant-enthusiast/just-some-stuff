# Kalos: The Last Warm Water
## Age Eight, Arc II — Guests at Low Water
### Revised narrative draft

**Status:** Canon-candidate literary draft. This version supersedes the earlier Arc II prose draft and implements the binding editorial direction in `kalos/docs/literary-prose-constitution.md`.

This version incorporates the approved conclusions:

- **The Borrowed Bed** precedes **Words Through the Wall**.
- Scenes contain more lived duration before and after decisions.
- Kalos remains socially perceptive but causally child-bound.
- Other people act without waiting for him.
- Aro has obligations and relationships that do not originate with Kalos.
- Quiet, preventive, and uneventful routes receive full narrative treatment.
- Player interaction extends to attention, provisional belief, narrative focus, and memory without becoming constant or intrusive.
- State effects are omitted from the prose draft; the engine should derive them separately.

Only one branch from each choice appears in a given playthrough.

# Scene Six
## Low Water

The rain had stopped before dawn. The settlement went on shedding it.

Water ticked from the roof edges and ran down the uprights of the drying racks. The paths were black with footprints. Beyond the last houses, the inlet had pulled back past the usual stones and left long bars of mud shining under the cloud. Gulls stood on them facing the wind.

Kalos carried Mara’s tray against his hip. The dry cord weighed almost nothing. That made it easy to forget that one fall into the mud would spoil half the morning’s work.

Pali walked beside him with a strip of crisped fish skin balanced across both palms. Eda had told her to take it to Seli. Pali kept her elbows close and watched the path with the strained attention of someone carrying a much larger thing.

“Do not run,” Kalos said.

“I am not.”

“You are thinking about it.”

Pali looked up at him. “You cannot see thinking.”

She turned her attention back to the fish skin and nearly stepped into a puddle.

The gull came from behind.

Kalos heard the wings before he saw the bird. Pali cried out. The tray slipped against his hip. By the time he caught it against his ribs, the fish skin was already in the gull’s beak.

The bird landed on the shed roof and swallowed.

Pali looked down at her empty hands.

“Make it give it back.”

“It swallowed.”

“Make it unswallow.”

The corner of the tray dipped. Three coils of cord began to slide.

Neri, working near the shed doorway, put out one foot beneath the tray and stopped it against his shin. He caught the loose cord before it touched the ground. His other hand still held the hook he had been binding.

He looked first at the cord, then at Kalos.

“You were holding that.”

“I still am.”

Neri returned the coils to the tray. “Not all of it.”

Pali had begun to cry without noise. Her mouth was open, but she seemed unwilling to give the gull anything else.

Kalos lowered the tray onto a dry plank.

Oren came from beneath the racks with two older children behind him. He looked from Pali to the gull, which had already become interested in something farther down the roof.

“It will come back if you hold another piece up,” Oren said.

Pali covered both hands against her chest.

“That is not how coming back works,” Neri said.

Oren ignored him. “You stand here, and when it comes low, Kalos catches it.”

“How?” Pali asked.

Oren glanced at Kalos, leaving the difficult part to him.

Kalos imagined the bird dropping close enough to seize. He imagined the older children watching. He imagined Pali carrying the recovered fish skin into the house and telling Eda how he had taken it from the gull’s beak.

The gull lifted away from the roof.

It did not circle. It crossed the yard, cleared the houses, and went toward the exposed flats.

Pali watched until it became one gray movement among several.

Then someone at the water shouted.

Not alarm. Announcement.

Two canoes had entered the inlet.

They came without song. The paddlers let the last of the falling water carry them until both hulls grounded in the mud well short of the usual landing. Bundles had been tied above the gunwales in uneven stacks. One canoe rode low enough that the adults on shore stopped speaking before they began moving.

Mara came out of the shed, wiping one hand against her work wrap.

“Take the cord inside,” she told Kalos.

Pali caught his tunic.

“I have to tell Eda.”

“You have nothing to take her.”

“I have to tell her that.”

The people from the canoes had begun testing the mud with paddles. An adult in the first boat stepped down and sank nearly to the knee.

Oren ran toward the shore.

“Do not step where it shines,” he called. “The inlet keeps visitors there.”

A child in the bow stood up.

She was around Neri’s age, perhaps a little older. Her hair had been cut bluntly at the jaw. She held a narrow bundle under one arm and used her free hand to keep a covered basket from sliding against the canoe’s side. The wet shoulder of her garment had gone nearly black.

She looked at the mud, then at Oren.

“Come show me.”

Oren stopped at the firm edge.

“I am not a visitor.”

The child adjusted the bundle higher beneath her arm. “Then it knows you.”

One of the visiting adults laughed. Oren’s face changed.

Kalos could hear the better line before he had decided whether to say it.

The mud had tried Oren yesterday and returned him.

The older children were looking toward him now. So was Neri. Pali still held the edge of his tunic. Behind them, Mara had gone back into the shed, apparently trusting the cord to continue existing wherever she had told Kalos to put it.

The bow rope fell into the mud, a body-length short of shore.

Three different needs opened at once.

## Route: Take the bow rope

Kalos set the tray on the driest plank he could find.

“Do not touch it,” he told Oren.

Oren stared at him. “I was not going to.”

“That is why I told you.”

A few of the children laughed. Not enough to restore the line Kalos had saved for the mud, but enough that Oren had to decide whether pursuing him would look worse than remaining.

Kalos stepped down from the stones.

The first layer of mud was cold and soft. Beneath it lay something firmer that tilted unexpectedly under his foot. His ankle disappeared. He pulled free and lost one of his soft footwraps with a sound like wet cloth being torn.

The child in the canoe leaned over the bow.

“Take the rope.”

“I know.”

“You are standing on it.”

Kalos looked down. The rope had gone beneath his left foot.

He freed it and took the end with both hands.

The nearest adult on shore came beside him. Oren followed after a pause and chose a route along the darker mud, where the gravel held closer to the surface.

“Here,” Oren said. “Not there.”

He was right.

They pulled together.

The canoe moved less than Kalos expected. The rope tightened into his palms. The adult beside him leaned back and used his weight rather than his arms. Kalos copied him. Oren moved sideways toward firmer ground, keeping the line clear of a stone.

The canoe shifted.

For one breath the entire hull seemed to balance on the mud. Then it slid forward hard enough to pull Kalos onto one knee.

Mud entered beneath his tunic and covered his shin. Someone in the canoe shouted. The child with the bundle braced the basket with her foot and caught an older woman beneath the elbow.

The older woman’s right palm had been wrapped in dark fiber. Rain had soaked the wrapping through.

When the canoe reached the stones, the child climbed out without waiting for help. She handed the narrow bundle to another visitor, then turned back for the basket.

She had not looked at Kalos since telling him he was standing on the rope.

Adults began unloading.

Sealed food bundles went first. Then rolled coverings, baskets of tools, a paddle with a split blade tied stiff between two narrow boards. Nothing was placed casually. Even the wet coverings were kept from the mud.

Kalos took one end of a bundle and discovered that it was heavier than its size suggested.

“Oil,” the woman with the wrapped palm said. “Keep it level.”

He shifted his grip.

Nearby, two adults from the visitors’ canoe spoke while they worked.

“The south roof went in before midnight.”

“What stayed sealed?”

“Not enough.”

No one lowered a voice for Kalos. No one explained the words either.

Aro—Kalos learned her name only after hearing someone call it twice—was counting the bundles as they left the canoe. She began again whenever another person moved one without telling her.

“Six,” she said.

“Seven,” Oren corrected.

Aro pointed. “That one is East Inlet’s.”

Oren looked at the coil of rope he had counted.

“I knew.”

Aro went on counting.

Kalos carried the oil bundle uphill. Mud dried cold along his shin. He wanted Aro to notice that he had been the first child into the water.

She was watching the sealed baskets.

## Route: Walk Pali back

Kalos took Pali’s hand.

“We will tell Eda before she sends someone looking for the fish skin.”

Pali did not move.

“The canoes came.”

“They will still be here.”

“What if they go?”

“They brought sleeping covers.”

Pali looked toward the crowded boats and considered this.

Kalos picked up Mara’s tray with his free hand. Pali allowed herself to be led, though she twisted twice to look behind them.

The nearer they came to the house, the more rapidly she improved the gull.

By the threshold, it had struck Kalos in the face.

Inside, it had been large enough to lift the whole strip without landing.

By the time Eda turned from the hearth, Kalos had nearly caught its leg.

Pali finished the story with both hands around Kalos’s wrist.

Eda looked at the empty hands, the clean tray, and the mud on neither child.

“You were trusted with food,” she said.

Pali’s lower lip moved.

Eda crouched.

“The gull took the food. Did it take the truth too?”

Pali shook her head.

“Then tell Seli what happened.”

Pali went to find her.

Eda remained crouched for a moment longer. “And the tray?”

“Neri caught it.”

“You thanked him?”

Kalos had not.

Eda stood before he could answer. Outside, the settlement had gathered around the grounded canoes.

“Take Mara’s cord where it belongs. Then help with the wet bedding.”

By the time Kalos reached the yard, Oren was already in the mud beside the first canoe. He had found a firm route and was directing two younger children away from the deeper places.

The visiting child with the blunt-cut hair stood on shore with her bundle. She watched Oren correct a man twice his age about where to place his foot. The man listened.

Kalos arrived carrying dry cord.

Pali came behind him and called his name.

The visiting child looked at Pali first.

Pali took Kalos’s hand again, though she no longer needed anything from him.

That was how Aro first understood their relation.

## Route: Finish Mara’s work

Kalos lifted the tray.

“I will come back,” he told Pali.

She had begun rubbing the palms that no longer held anything.

“Come now.”

“Mara told me.”

Pali looked toward the gulls on the flats. None was the same bird anymore.

“You always do what Mara says after something happens.”

Kalos did not know whether this was true. The tray had become heavier against his hip.

He carried it into the shed.

Mara stood over a low rack of line, testing each length between her hands. She did not look up when he entered.

“Put it above the oil tray.”

He did.

“The visitors’ south roof failed,” Mara said.

Kalos waited.

“That means more work here.”

She handed him a sealed bundle of completed hooks.

“Take these to Eda.”

“I can help pull the canoes.”

“Other people are already pulling them.”

Kalos looked toward the doorway.

Mara tested another line. “Do not stop to become useful to someone else.”

The sentence irritated him because she spoke as if usefulness were a place he wandered into for pleasure.

Outside, the children shouted. A canoe struck the stones. Someone laughed when a visitor stepped too deep.

Kalos carried the hooks to the house.

Eda had begun moving bowls from a storage shelf to the long work surface. Seli was rolling bedding and tying each bundle with different fiber so the household could tell its own coverings from the visitors’ once everything was wet.

Kalos placed the hooks where Eda pointed.

A few moments later, Aro entered backward, pulling one side of a heavy basket with Neri on the other. Her hair had dried against one cheek. She looked at the hooks, at Kalos, and at the space Eda cleared for the basket.

“Here,” Eda said.

Aro and Neri lowered it together.

No one asked who Kalos was.

For the first time that morning, he had arrived too late to alter the first account of himself.

## Attention interaction

The canoes are unloaded. The house and yard continue changing.

> **What does Kalos keep watching?**

### The sealed food bundles

The visitors had brought fewer food bundles than bedding.

Kalos counted twice because the answer felt wrong. People who intended to stay should have brought more food than coverings. Yet every sealed basket was handled as though dropping it would change the winter.

One bundle had been wrapped in three layers and tied with new cord. Another showed a damp patch no larger than Kalos’s hand. The visiting woman with the wrapped palm touched that patch each time anyone moved the bundle.

Veya came from the house and crouched beside it.

She pressed the wrapping, smelled her fingers, and said nothing.

The woman said, “Only the outer layer.”

Veya looked up.

The woman corrected herself. “We think.”

### Aro’s narrow bundle

Aro had surrendered every basket except the narrow bundle.

It remained beneath her arm while she worked. When Eda told her she could put it inside, Aro asked where. Eda pointed near the hearth. Aro shook her head. Eda pointed near the sleeping wall. Aro shook her head again.

At last Seli showed her the high ledge above the stored mats.

Aro tested the wood before placing the bundle there.

“What is it?” Pali asked.

“Dry,” Aro said.

Pali waited for the real answer.

Aro had already turned away.

### Seli

When one of the visiting adults spoke of the upper river, Seli stopped tying a bedding roll.

Only for a moment. The cord remained between her teeth.

The adult mentioned a channel Kalos did not know and a place where red fiber could be made without smoke. Veya replied with the name of another place.

Seli finished the knot too tightly. She had to undo it.

Eda saw.

Neither of them spoke.

### Neri

Neri began sorting the visitors’ wet tools without being asked.

He placed wood beside wood, fiber in a dry bowl, stone edges away from the children. An adult moved one item from his arrangement. Neri waited until the adult turned, then moved it back.

Mara saw him do this.

She did not correct him.

Kalos wondered how long Neri had been trusted in rooms where no one said his name.

## Convergence

By midday, the visitors’ coverings occupied one side of the communal house.

Seli’s dyed fiber had disappeared beneath someone else’s wet wrap. Pali’s bedding was folded on top of Kalos’s. Eda counted the bowls, stopped, and counted again.

Aro’s bundle remained on the high ledge.

The visitors did not ask where they would sleep.

No one at East Inlet said how long they would stay.

The house made room before either answer was given.

# Scene Seven
## The Sweet Oil

The visitors’ gift was a pouch no longer than Eda’s hand.

It had been sewn from pale, thin skin and stopped with a plug of dark wood. The woman with the wrapped palm carried it to Veya herself. She set it beside the elder rather than handing it to Eda, which meant the gift belonged to a conversation as well as a meal.

Pali climbed onto both knees.

Eda pressed two fingers to her shoulder until she sat again.

Veya worked the plug loose with her thumbnail.

The smell reached the children before the pouch changed hands. It was cleaner than tool oil and heavier than broth, with something sweet beneath the smoke.

Eda used the back of a bone spoon. One bright stroke across each adult bowl. A smaller one for the children. No one asked whether the portions were equal. Everyone watched closely enough to know they were not.

Kalos held his bowl near his face.

The oil spread in a thin shine over the fish. It gathered at the grain of the wood and made the broth move away from it.

Across from him, Aro had not touched hers.

She had set the spoon sideways across the bowl. A bead of oil traveled slowly down the handle toward her thumb.

The woman with the wrapped palm sat three places away, speaking with Veya. She had received an adult portion but had eaten nothing.

Aro glanced at her, then back at the spoon.

Oren had already swallowed his.

He licked the side of his thumb and said the oil from East Inlet’s south rocks was better.

“You do not make this oil,” Seli said.

“I have eaten it.”

“That is not making.”

Oren looked toward Kalos, inviting assistance.

Kalos could improve either side.

## Attention interaction

> **What does Kalos keep watching while the meal continues?**

### The shine inside his bowl

The oil gathered in places the spoon had not touched.

Each time Kalos tilted the bowl, a small bright line moved through the broth and joined another. He could have chased it for several mouthfuls if no one had been watching.

Pali’s bowl had less. She had begun scraping the edge with one finger.

### Aro’s untouched spoon

Aro waited until Veya turned toward Eda.

Then she lifted her spoon and passed it behind the next person to the woman with the wrapped palm.

The woman frowned.

Aro held the spoon there.

After a moment the woman ate from it and gave the spoon back.

Aro wiped the handle against her own bowl as if the exchange had been ordinary.

### Eda counting bowls

Eda’s eyes moved through the room faster than her hands.

She knew who had not been served, who had received an adult measure by mistake, who had given food away, which visitor was pretending to be finished, and which child had exchanged bowls with someone larger.

Twice she counted the remaining portions.

The second time, she looked toward the sealed food bundles.

### Veya’s hand on the pouch

Veya kept one hand resting over the pouch after the portions were served.

The visiting adults spoke of the south roof, wet stores, and the bay beside East Inlet’s smokehouse. Whenever one of them made a request, Veya’s fingers tightened slightly around the pale skin.

The pouch was a gift.

It also kept the conversation from becoming only a request.

## Minor interaction: Kalos’s portion

> **What does he do with what is his?**

### Eat it

Kalos touched his tongue to the fish.

Salt first, then smoke, then warmth. By the time he swallowed, the taste had changed into the memory of itself.

He ate the second bite more slowly. It did not last longer.

His tongue returned to the inside of his cheek, then to the rim of the bowl.

Nothing followed.

The meal continued. Pali complained about a bone. Oren began a story he had told twice before. Eda asked for another bowl.

Kalos had eaten something rare and good.

No later event arrived to justify or condemn it.

### Share it with Pali

Kalos tipped his spoon toward Pali’s bowl.

She watched the oil slide.

“That is too much,” she said, which meant she wanted him to continue.

He scraped half the spoon against her fish.

Pali immediately ate the entire piece. She closed her eyes to hold the taste in place and opened them again when it was already gone.

“More.”

“That was mine.”

“You gave it.”

“I gave that part.”

Pali considered the distinction. “Give the next part.”

Kalos ate what remained.

His portion was smaller. Pali leaned against his arm for the rest of the meal, warm and oily around the mouth.

### Keep it a little longer

Kalos left the spoon across his bowl.

Oren finished his story. A visitor answered a question. Eda moved past carrying hot stones.

The oil remained available.

That became part of its pleasure.

When Kalos finally ate, the fish had cooled. The oil had begun to thicken against the spoon. The taste was no richer than before, but he had possessed the possibility longer.

Across from him, Aro’s spoon was already empty.

### Offer it in trade

Kalos leaned toward Aro.

“What is in the bundle?”

Aro glanced at the ledge above the mats.

“Things.”

“I will give you this.”

She looked at the oil.

“It is yours already.”

“That is why I can give it.”

Aro touched the edge of her bowl with one finger. “I do not want it.”

“You saved yours.”

“I gave it away.”

Kalos had not seen to whom.

Aro looked toward Seli. “You can ask me something else.”

“What?”

“That is the thing you ask.”

The exchange pleased neither of them enough to end it.

Aro was helping the woman with the wrapped palm loosen the wet binding. Pali had gone under the work surface looking for a dropped bone. Seli and Veya were arguing quietly over where the visitors’ food could be stored.

No one stood between Kalos and the far side of the house.

## Route: Carry it directly

Kalos carried the pouch across the house.

The smell reached him through the stopped opening. It seemed stronger now that none had been given to him.

Veya was speaking with two visiting adults. Kalos waited beside her until she finished the sentence. She did not see the pouch at first.

He could have set it down.

Eda had said her hands.

He held it there.

At last Veya looked.

“What?”

Kalos lifted the pouch.

She took it.

The exchange was complete.

Veya resumed speaking before Kalos had stepped away.

He remained for one breath longer, waiting for someone to acknowledge that the pouch contained less because he had not taken more.

No one did.

He returned to the bowls.

Eda gave him three to carry outside for washing. She did not inspect the pouch or ask how he had crossed the room.

Later, when the visitors’ wet cord had to be moved away from the hearth, she sent Kalos with Seli rather than following to make sure it was done.

The trust arrived disguised as additional work.

Kalos did not recognize it immediately.

## Route: Ask Eda

Kalos did not move.

Eda had turned to the next task. He waited until she looked back.

“What?”

“Can I have one more taste?”

“No.”

She reached for a bowl.

Kalos kept holding the pouch.

Eda looked at him again.

“There is enough left for what it was brought to do.”

“What is that?”

“Not feed you twice.”

A visiting adult heard and smiled into her bowl. Heat climbed into Kalos’s face.

Eda took the nearest unwashed bowl and held it out.

“When you bring that back clean, scrape this one before you wash it.”

The bowl contained a streak of oil mixed with cold broth and fish skin.

It was not the same as another portion. It was still good.

Kalos carried the pouch to Veya. When he returned, Eda had not forgotten the bowl.

He scraped it with one finger behind the house, where no one was watching and no one needed to be deceived.

## Route: Take another taste

Kalos crossed behind the hanging partition near the storage recess.

The room did not become silent. No one turned toward him. The opportunity was smaller than the feeling it created.

He lifted the plug with his thumbnail.

The oil beneath it shone almost black.

He put one finger inside.

The second taste was larger than the first because he chose its size himself. It touched the back of his tongue before he was ready and made him swallow.

Someone moved behind him.

Aro stood at the end of the partition with a strip of damp wrapping in her hands.

She looked at Kalos’s finger.

Then at the pouch.

“Did Eda say you could?”

Kalos replaced the plug.

“I am taking it to Veya.”

“That is not what I asked.”

The words were not clever. Aro sounded tired.

Kalos wiped his finger against the inside of his mouth.

The woman with the injured palm called Aro’s name.

Aro looked toward her.

Kalos could have explained. He could have offered a taste, denied what she had seen, or told her it was common at East Inlet.

She left before he chose.

He placed the pouch in Veya’s hands.

No one counted the oil that night.

The second taste remained entirely good.

What changed was that Aro now knew where he had taken it.

What she believed about the taking was still her own.

## Scene residue

After the children were told to settle, the adults remained upright.

Small requests were often made while people worked. This one survived the meal, the bowls, the rearranged firewood, and three efforts by Eda to send everyone toward sleep.

Kalos lay down before the answer came.

The smell of the oil remained inside the nearest wooden bowl.

In the morning, after Eda scrubbed it with ash, Kalos could still find the place where the grain had darkened.

# Scene Eight
## The Borrowed Bed

The visitors stayed.

No one announced this. Their wet coverings remained near the fire after dark. Their food bundles moved from the doorway to the wall. Eda began speaking of tomorrow’s work with their adults included.

That was how staying became true.

The house had enough floor for everyone if no one required the place used the night before.

Eda and Seli had already begun moving bedding by the time Kalos understood that his own place was involved.

Seli lost the wall first.

Her rolled covering was carried toward the center, where people would have to step around her to reach the fire. She objected once. Eda said there were three people older than Seli and one with an injured hand. Seli stopped arguing and began moving other people’s things with more force than necessary.

Pali had been placed beside Eda.

She crawled out before the covering was unrolled.

“I sleep with Kalos.”

“You did yesterday,” Eda said.

“And before.”

“You began beside Veya before.”

“I finished with Kalos.”

“That is true,” Veya said from the wall. “It is not helpful.”

Kalos’s place lay between a support post and the stacked winter mats. The post stopped people from rolling onto him; the mats stopped people from stepping across him. It was warmer than the doorway and private enough that he could turn toward the wall when he wanted not to be part of the house.

Aro’s narrow bundle now occupied the space.

She had placed it against the post and covered it with the driest half of her shoulder wrap. She stood nearby holding two rolled coverings while Seli argued with Eda about where the visitors’ injured woman could lie without being struck by cold air from the door.

Kalos touched the bundle.

Aro looked over.

“Do not.”

“That is my place.”

“It is dry.”

“So is the ledge.”

“The ledge drips now.”

A line of water had begun descending the wall where the rain found a roof seam. Each drop struck the wood above the ledge and spread.

“What is in it?” Kalos asked.

Aro tightened her grip on the bedding. “Something that should stay dry.”

Pali came between them and put both arms around Kalos’s waist.

“I sleep here.”

“There is no here yet,” Seli said.

Oren, who did not sleep in this house, had remained long enough to enjoy the confusion.

“Visitors should go near the door,” he said. “They are used to weather.”

Eda turned.

“Would you like to show them?”

Oren remembered that someone outside needed him.

Pali crawled beneath one of the unrolled coverings and disappeared.

“I am an otter,” she said.

“No animals,” Eda answered.

Pali’s head emerged. “Then I am Pali under a covering.”

“That is worse. Come out.”

Aro laughed before covering her mouth.

It was the first time Kalos had heard her laugh.

She did not look at him when she did it.

## Route: Keep the place

“I sleep there,” Kalos said.

Eda was kneeling over a bedding roll. She looked up.

“I know.”

“I want to keep it.”

The house did not become silent, but the people nearest him heard.

Kalos wished he had phrased it differently. There was no better phrasing that changed what he wanted.

Eda looked toward the doorway, the injured visitor, Aro’s bundle, and the space beside Veya.

“All right,” she said.

Aro lifted her bundle before Kalos touched it again.

Eda moved an adult toward the doorway and placed Aro beside Seli. The injured woman received the warmer place near Veya. Pali was returned to Eda’s bedding and escaped once more before being caught by Seli.

The arrangement required three more movements because Kalos did not move.

No one called him selfish. No one congratulated his honesty.

He crawled into his place.

Pali came after the fire had been lowered. She stepped over Aro, crossed Seli’s covering, and pushed herself between Kalos and the winter mats.

“You were put with Eda,” Kalos whispered.

“I was.”

“Go back.”

Pali closed her eyes.

Kalos remained warm.

Across the house, Aro and Seli spoke quietly. Their words were too low to follow. Aro laughed once. Seli answered with something that made her pull the covering over her mouth.

Kalos listened until the spaces between their words grew longer.

In the morning, Aro found him awake.

“You said you wanted it,” she said.

“Yes.”

She nodded as though confirming a fact about the weather.

She did not seem offended.

Seli was.

“You could have moved.”

“I did not want to.”

“I know.”

She tied her hair back and left to help Veya before Kalos could decide whether the answer had been criticism.

## Route: Give it up

“Aro can put it there,” Kalos said.

Aro looked at him.

“The bundle,” he added.

Eda moved past carrying two coverings. “Then choose another place before someone chooses it for you.”

Kalos took his bedding toward the doorway.

The cold entered beneath the lower plank and spread across the floor. He laid his covering close enough to the wall that no one could step between him and it.

Pali followed.

Eda returned her.

When Kalos looked back, Aro had set the narrow bundle carefully between the post and the winter mats. She covered it with her driest layer.

Then she took her bedding and lay beside Seli near the center of the house.

“You can sleep there too,” Kalos said.

Aro looked toward the dry place.

“I thought you meant the bundle.”

“I meant you.”

“You said the bundle.”

She lay down beside Seli.

Kalos settled near the doorway.

The first hour felt brave. The second felt cold.

Someone crossed him in the night and stepped on the edge of his covering. Pali woke and called for Eda. One of the visiting adults coughed until Veya raised the fire.

Kalos watched the dry place. Aro’s bundle remained there, untouched and ungrateful.

Near dawn he slept.

When he woke, his knees ached from holding them close to his chest.

Aro had folded the dry half of his covering over him. He knew it was her because the fold was wrong for Eda and too careful for Pali.

She did not mention it.

Neither did he.

---

## Route: Ask Seli

Kalos stepped away from the post.

“Seli.”

She had one end of a heavy covering in her teeth.

“What?”

“Where should everyone go?”

Seli dropped the covering.

Eda looked toward them but did not interfere.

Seli stood in the middle of the house and examined the floor.

“Aro beside me. Her bundle stays dry there.” She pointed to Kalos’s place. “The injured woman goes by Veya. Pali goes with Eda. Kalos keeps the post because he kicks.”

“I do not.”

“You kicked me yesterday.”

“You were on my covering.”

Seli continued. “The two men go near the door. Move the tool basket away from the wall before it leaks.”

She began moving people before anyone had agreed.

The arrangement worked.

Aro placed her bundle beside Kalos’s mat and slept near Seli. Pali complained until Eda gave her a wooden spoon to hold. The visiting adults near the door used their rolled garments to stop the cold air beneath the plank.

Kalos ended where he had begun.

He had neither sacrificed nor defended his place.

That should have made the result easier.

Instead he lay awake waiting for someone to call his name.

No one did.

Seli and Aro spoke near the center of the house. Pali whispered to the spoon. Eda and Veya settled an adult disagreement without asking Kalos to make anyone laugh.

The arrangement continued to work.

By morning, Kalos had slept well.

He was still irritated.

---

## Optional route: The animal nests

Pali crawled beneath the covering again.

“I am an otter.”

Kalos looked at the piles of bedding, the wet visitors, and Eda’s face.

“Otters sleep near water.”

Pali went still.

“Herons get the wall,” Kalos continued. “Seals go near the fire because they complain when they are cold.”

“I do not complain,” Oren said from the doorway.

“You are a gull.”

The room decided this was correct.

Oren objected. Pali emerged from the covering to flap both arms and steal an imaginary fish from his hand. Aro chose a heron after rejecting fox, seal, and dog. Seli said she was not an animal and moved two bedding rolls while everyone argued.

The game gave the children a reason to carry coverings without being asked.

It also put one adult’s bedding in the wrong place and buried a wooden bowl beneath a mat.

Eda found the bowl.

She looked at Kalos.

He helped restore the room without waiting for her to ask.

By the time everyone lay down, Pali had forgotten which animal she was and crawled beside Eda.

The work had been made easier.

There had also been more of it.

## With Seli and Aro

Seli unrolled Aro’s shoulder covering near the fire.

The outer edge had been dyed almost black. Beneath it the fibers were red, though smoke and rain had dulled them.

Seli touched the inside seam.

“What did you use?”

Aro pulled the covering away.

Seli looked at her.

Aro spread it again.

“My mother does not tell people after one question.”

“I asked you.”

“That is worse.”

Seli turned the cloth toward the firelight. “It does not smell smoked.”

“It is not.”

Veya, speaking with adults near the wall, glanced toward them and then away.

Aro lowered her voice.

“There is a place upriver where the roots grow red beneath the black ones.”

They bent over the garment together.

Neither noticed when Kalos returned.

## With Neri and Veya

Veya opened one of the visitors’ damp food bundles.

Neri held the wrapping apart.

Inside, the upper layer of dried fish had softened. The pieces beneath remained hard.

“How much?” Veya asked.

Neri counted.

“Twenty-three good.”

Veya took out one piece and turned it over.

“Count the undersides.”

Neri did.

Three had darkened where moisture collected beneath them.

“Twenty.”

Veya nodded.

Neri’s face tightened. He had not been careless. He had used the wrong sign.

Veya did not tell him he was usually better than this.

She handed him the next bundle.

He changed the way he counted.

## With Oren at the canoe

The second canoe had been pulled above the tide line.

Everyone else had gone inside.

Oren crouched beside a rib where the wood had begun to separate. He pressed it with his thumb, looked toward the houses, and then fetched two narrow offcuts from the shed.

He tied them across the split with wet cord.

The first binding slipped.

He undid it and began again.

No one watched him finish.

In the morning, one of the visiting adults found the brace and called for whoever had made it.

Oren was already beneath the drying racks, telling younger children about the mud.

He did not hear.

# Scene Nine
## Words Through the Wall

The house was too full for sleep to arrive normally.

Every person’s breathing changed someone else’s bedding. A cough near the doorway woke Pali near the fire. Someone rolled onto a wet covering and swore quietly. The visitors’ unfamiliar sleep sounds made the house seem larger and more crowded at once.

Pali’s heel rested against Kalos’s shin.

He moved it.

She put it back.

The adults spoke beyond the hanging partition near the storage recess. The cloth kept out the firelight, not the words.

Veya said, “The south bay stays dry.”

Someone answered too softly.

Eda said, “Through first ice?”

A pause.

Then Seli’s name.

Kalos opened his eyes.

The fire had gone low enough that the roof beams disappeared. Pali slept with one fist closed around the edge of his tunic.

Beyond the partition, a visitor said, “She knows the upper channels.”

Veya answered.

Kalos heard only the last words:

“—if she chooses.”

Another voice said, “One child is easier than four.”

The covering beside Kalos moved.

Seli was awake.

He could not tell whether she had heard.

## Route: Wake Seli

Kalos reached across Pali and touched Seli’s shoulder.

Her eyes opened before his hand settled.

“They said your name,” he whispered.

“I heard.”

“They said one child.”

Seli looked toward the partition.

“Do they mean Pali?”

“No.”

“Then who?”

“Me, maybe.”

Kalos waited for her to sound afraid.

She did not.

“You do not have to go.”

Seli turned her face toward him.

“I asked Veya.”

The answer did not fit the danger Kalos had built.

“Why?”

“Because I want to see the upper water.”

“You have seen the river.”

“Not that part.”

“You could go and come back.”

“That is generally how going works.”

Kalos almost laughed. He did not want Seli to think the question was funny.

“How long?”

“I do not know.”

“Eda will not let you.”

“I did not ask Eda.”

Pali shifted between them and tightened her hand around Kalos’s tunic.

Beyond the partition, Eda said Seli’s name again. This time the tone made clear that Seli had been part of the conversation before Kalos woke.

“You already knew,” Kalos said.

Seli rolled onto her back.

“Yes.”

“How long?”

“I do not know that either.”

He wanted her to explain the whole thing now that he had found it. She closed her eyes.

“I am trying to sleep.”

Kalos lay beside her, fully awake.

Seli had not been taken from the household. She had reached beyond it with a question.

The distinction did not make the space beside him feel less temporary.

## Route: Keep listening

Kalos held still.

The adults’ words became clearer when he stopped searching each one for Pali or Seli.

The visitors needed the south bay of East Inlet’s storehouse. Their own roof had failed. Some food had spoiled. What remained might last through the first ice if it stayed dry.

Then Seli returned to the conversation.

A visiting adult said she had asked about the upper channels during an earlier meeting.

Eda said, “She is thirteen.”

Veya answered, “She will still be thirteen in spring.”

Someone shifted behind the partition.

The cloth lifted at one corner.

Veya looked directly at Kalos.

“Since you are awake, sit up.”

Kalos obeyed.

Pali’s hand remained closed around his tunic. He had to loosen her fingers one at a time.

The adults made a space for him near the edge of the fire. It was not a place of authority. It was a place where they could see whether he continued listening.

Eda held out a bowl of water.

“What did you hear?”

Kalos considered improving the amount.

“The south bay. First ice. Seli.”

“That is enough to misunderstand several things,” Eda said.

One of the visitors smiled. Veya did not.

Eda told him that the visitors needed dry storage. She said Seli had asked Veya about a spring journey. Nothing had been decided.

“Does she want to go?”

“That is a question for Seli.”

“Do you want her to?”

Eda looked toward the bedding.

“That is not a question for tonight.”

Kalos disliked the answer because it sounded complete while leaving the part he wanted untouched.

Veya sent him back to sleep.

In the morning, he remembered the conversation as longer than it had been.

---

## Route: Ask now

Kalos sat up.

The movement pulled Pali’s fist against his tunic and woke her halfway.

“Where is Seli going?” he asked.

The adult conversation stopped.

Seli answered from the bedding.

“Nowhere tonight.”

One of the visitors laughed. Eda did not.

Kalos looked toward the partition. “They said your name.”

“They know it,” Seli said.

Eda moved the cloth aside.

“No one is being sent anywhere,” she said.

“Then why are you talking about one child?”

The visiting woman with the wrapped palm shifted her hand beneath the covering.

Veya spoke before Eda.

“Because four people require more food and bedding than one. That does not mean we are exchanging children in the dark.”

Pali sat up.

“Who is exchanging children?”

“No one,” Eda said.

“Which child?”

“No child.”

Pali looked at Kalos.

He had created a question large enough to wake her and no answer simple enough to put her back to sleep.

Eda came through the partition and lay Pali down again.

To Kalos she said, “Possible is not decided. Decided is not begun.”

“What is possible?”

“Seli may travel in spring.”

“Why?”

Seli pulled her covering over her head.

Eda looked at the shape of her beneath it.

“Ask her in the morning.”

The adults resumed speaking more quietly.

Kalos lay down.

The partition now seemed better at keeping out words than before.

---

## Route: Promise Pali

Kalos turned toward Pali.

Her eyes remained closed.

He whispered, “You are staying here.”

Pali opened them.

“Here?”

“Yes.”

“With you?”

“Yes.”

The answer entered her before she was fully awake. Her fist loosened around his tunic.

“Good,” she said.

She slept.

Kalos remained awake with the promise between them.

In the morning, Pali carried it to Eda.

“Kalos said I stay here.”

Eda stopped folding a covering.

“Did he?”

Pali nodded.

Eda looked at Kalos.

The adult conversation had not concerned Pali. No one intended to move her. The promise was therefore easy to keep for reasons Kalos had not known when he made it.

Eda did not call him a liar.

She said, “Do not give away what is not yours to guarantee.”

Pali had already gone to show Aro where the bowls were kept.

---

## Scene residue

Rain began again near dawn.

It found the repaired roof seam above the storage wall. Water gathered there, held, and moved sideways toward the eave.

Kalos watched until the last drop disappeared.

Beside him, Seli slept facing away.

# Scene Ten
## The Cave at Half Tide

This scene is conditional. Some chronicles receive the cave route. Others receive the tide-pool route. Both should carry equal narrative weight.

---

# Route A
## The Pools Below the Point

Pali followed them badly.

She attempted silence by placing each foot with exaggerated care and then running whenever she thought the distance had grown too large. Her oversized tunic caught the wind. Twice she stopped to inspect shells. Each time she looked up and found the older children farther away.

Kalos saw her before anyone else did.

Aro was ahead with Neri, discussing the tide line. Oren carried a coil of cord and had begun telling Aro which paths remained dry longest, though she had used the western point before he had.

Pali crouched behind a drift log.

Only the top of her hair remained visible.

Kalos stopped.

Neri looked back.

“What?”

Kalos pointed.

Pali lowered her head. This made the hair disappear but left the rest of her exposed.

Oren laughed.

“She can wait at the pools.”

“She cannot come to the cave,” Neri said.

“I can,” Pali called.

Aro studied the distance back to the settlement.

“We will lose the low water.”

Pali stood and walked toward them before anyone invited her.

Kalos knew the cave would be open for less than an hour.

He also knew that returning Pali to the house would take most of it.

No adult had assigned her to him. No one else stepped toward her.

## Stay among the tide pools

Kalos told the others to go.

Oren looked pleased too quickly.

Aro did not ask whether Kalos was afraid. She handed him one of Neri’s pale marker shells.

“For the high line.”

Neri pointed toward the darker band on the stones.

“Do not stay below that when the pools begin joining.”

Then they went around the point.

Pali watched until they disappeared.

“You wanted to go.”

“Yes.”

“You can.”

“No.”

“Why?”

Kalos looked at her.

Pali picked up a stone and threw it into the nearest pool. The water struck a low shelf and returned in a small cold wave over her foot.

She screamed, then laughed.

The pools extended along the black stone in different shapes and depths. Some held only water and weed. Others contained worlds dense enough that Pali lay on her stomach to see them.

A small crab raised both claws beneath a rock.

“Oren,” Pali said.

“That is not Oren.”

“It does the same.”

She moved one finger toward the crab. It shifted sideways without lowering either claw.

Pali moved her finger sideways too.

They remained like that until the crab disappeared beneath the rock.

Farther down, a sea star clung to the shaded wall of a pool. Its upper surface was the dull color of old blood. Beneath the water, hundreds of pale feet moved so slowly Kalos could not see the motion until he looked away and back.

Pali touched the water but not the animal.

“Does it know it is moving?”

Kalos did not know.

He said, “Probably.”

Pali accepted this.

The tide reached the first shell.

It tipped once in the wash and settled again.

Kalos moved it higher.

They found a fish no longer than Pali’s hand trapped in a shallow hollow. Each returning wave covered it; each retreat left its back in the air. Pali wanted to carry it to a larger pool.

Kalos lifted it carefully. The fish struck his palm hard enough to escape and landed in the same hollow.

Pali laughed until she could not stand.

On the second attempt they moved it.

It vanished beneath dark weed without acknowledgment.

When Aro, Neri, and Oren returned around the point, their legs were wet to the knee. Oren had a new scrape along one forearm. Aro spoke rapidly about green light, a high chamber, and a line of driftwood above Kalos’s head.

Pali interrupted to tell them about the fish.

Oren said the cave had been better.

Pali said, “The fish hit Kalos.”

Aro looked at the wet mark on Kalos’s tunic.

“Hard?”

Pali demonstrated against his arm.

The cave remained a place Kalos had not seen.

The pools did not become lesser because of it.

---

## Take Pali home

Pali objected the entire way back.

She said she could climb. She said the tide liked her. She said Eda had not forbidden the cave because Eda did not know about it.

At the last house, Pali stopped.

“I will not tell.”

“That is not the problem.”

“What is?”

Kalos could not explain it without making himself sound like an adult.

He took her to Eda.

By the time he returned to the shore, the western point had closed. Water covered the path in short white strikes.

He sat on the stones and waited.

The others came back by the upper route much later, wet and exhilarated.

Aro’s account began before she reached him.

Kalos listened.

The cave became detailed inside him without ever becoming memory.

---

## Bring her to the entrance

Pali made it around the point without falling.

At the cave mouth, the stone lowered until even Kalos had to bend. Water moved in and out across the floor.

Pali stopped.

The sound inside was larger than the opening.

“I wait here,” she said.

“No.”

“I do.”

“You cannot wait alone.”

Pali sat on a dry stone and folded both arms.

Aro looked toward the green chamber beyond.

Neri looked toward the tide.

Oren said, “We can see her from the first room.”

They could not.

Kalos had brought the choice this far without making it easier.

The engine should permit several resolutions here: Kalos remains with Pali; the whole group abandons the cave; Oren volunteers to stay and unexpectedly does so well; or they return together. Entering while leaving Pali alone is possible but creates a serious breach that later prose must encounter rather than merely score.

# Route B
## The Green Chamber

Aro asked Neri about the tide before she spoke to Kalos.

They stood above the hard strip near the racks, each holding one end of a length of cord. Neri had drawn marks into the mud with a bone point. Aro corrected one of them. Neri erased it and drew another.

Oren arrived carrying six pale shells.

“I brought markers.”

Neri looked at the shells. “Those are mine.”

“I brought them from where you left them.”

“That is not the same.”

Oren gave him the shells.

Aro had been to the cave once, during a journey when her household waited for the tide to turn. She knew the rooms. Neri knew East Inlet’s water. Oren knew the upper path back if the lower stones flooded.

Kalos knew none of those things.

He went anyway.

The western point changed as they approached. What looked from the settlement like one dark cliff separated into ledges, cuts, and narrow routes. Weed lay flat against the stone where water had recently left it. Small pools caught the color of the cloud.

Neri placed the first shell above the wet line.

Oren moved it higher.

“The water reached there yesterday,” he said.

Neri examined the darker band on the stone and moved the shell again.

Neither thanked the other.

At the cave entrance, Aro went first.

The ceiling lowered. Kalos put one hand against the stone and felt cold water moving beneath the skin of it.

The first chamber was gray.

The second was green.

Light entered through a submerged opening and traveled across the ceiling in slow broken bands. Each small wave changed the stone. Nothing in the chamber remained the same color for more than a breath.

No one spoke.

Even Oren let the silence stand.

Aro moved to the black shelf at the far side. Beyond it lay a higher chamber where old driftwood had been thrown above the ordinary tide line. She wanted to see whether one long branch remained where her aunt remembered it.

“If it moved,” she said, “the winter water came higher.”

Neri looked back toward the entrance.

“The second shell is wet.”

“It was wet when we came,” Oren said.

“No.”

Oren crouched beside it.

He touched the stone, then the shell.

“He is right.”

Neri looked toward the green light, which had shifted lower on the wall.

Aro had already climbed halfway onto the shelf.

## Remain with Neri

Kalos stayed beside the second shell.

Aro climbed over the shelf. Oren followed after telling Neri they would be one moment.

Neri said, “There is no one moment.”

They disappeared.

Water entered the chamber and withdrew. The shell shifted slightly.

Neri moved it.

“You said it was already wet,” Kalos said.

“I was wrong about where I put the first one.”

He did not lower his voice or explain why the mistake should not count.

Kalos watched the entrance.

“Call them.”

Neri called Aro’s name.

The cave returned it from two directions.

Oren answered first.

Neri called again. Louder.

Kalos knew how Oren would repeat the sound later. He would widen the fear inside it. He would make Neri the child who panicked in the cave.

The shell rolled.

Kalos caught it.

He called too.

Aro appeared above the shelf. “We found it.”

“Come back,” Neri said.

“The branch is still there.”

“The water is not.”

Aro looked toward the entrance and saw the change.

She climbed down.

Oren followed. He did not accuse Neri of fear. He took the cord and tied it around the slickest handhold before the others crossed.

They left while the lower path remained open.

Outside, Aro stood looking back at the cave.

“We could have stayed longer.”

Neri picked up the first shell.

“Yes.”

He did not say they should have.

On the return path, none of them needed rescue.

The green light continued moving after they left.

---

## Follow Aro

Kalos climbed the black shelf after Aro.

The stone was slick enough that his first foot returned to the floor. Oren showed him a narrow crack higher on the wall.

“There.”

Kalos used it.

The higher chamber smelled of old salt and dry wood. Drift branches lay wedged far above the current water. Some had been worn smooth. Others remained broken and pale where they struck the stone.

Aro went directly to the longest branch.

A dark strip had been tied around one end. The fiber had faded, but it remained.

She touched it.

“My aunt said this was above her head.”

“It is above yours,” Kalos said.

“She is taller.”

Aro looked at the line of old weed and sand beneath the branch.

The winter water had not moved it.

The information seemed to disappoint her.

Kalos had expected discovery to produce more.

Aro crouched and checked the tie.

“She thought it would be gone.”

“Why?”

“She thinks every place changes when we leave.”

Kalos looked toward the opening below. Neri’s voice reached them faintly.

“Maybe it does.”

Aro pulled at the fiber. It broke in her hand.

She stared at the two ends.

Then she put them beneath the branch where they had been.

Neri called again.

Aro rose.

On the way down, a wave crossed the lower floor and covered the place where Kalos had first stood. The water was not yet dangerous. It was simply earlier than he expected.

Oren had tied the cord around the slick handhold.

Kalos used it.

Outside, Aro told Neri the branch had remained. Neri asked where the water line was. She showed him against her shoulder.

They began arguing over whether that meant anything for the coming winter.

Kalos had been present when Aro found what she came to find.

She and Neri understood its use more clearly than he did.

---

## Follow Oren

Oren pointed toward a narrow opening beyond the green chamber.

“There is air moving.”

Kalos could feel it too, cold against the wet side of his face.

Aro said, “That does not go through.”

“You went there?”

“No.”

“Then you do not know.”

Oren climbed.

Kalos followed.

The passage narrowed after three body-lengths. Its floor sloped toward a dark pool. Air moved through a crack above them, not an exit.

Oren reached the end first.

He did not pretend otherwise.

“No opening,” he said.

A wave entered the passage and covered the lowest foothold.

Kalos turned.

The slope that had been easy downward required both hands going back. Water moved around his ankles and pulled at the loose end of his footwrap.

Oren climbed past him.

“Put your foot where my knee was.”

Kalos did.

The hold was there.

Behind them, another wave struck.

Oren reached the wider chamber and turned back. He took Kalos’s wrist, then let go as soon as Kalos found the shelf.

When they returned, Neri was furious.

“I called.”

“We heard,” Oren said.

“Then why did you not answer?”

“I was climbing.”

“You have a mouth while climbing.”

Oren looked at the water crossing the chamber floor.

He took the cord from his shoulder and tied it around the handhold without being asked.

His decision had wasted time.

His skill made the return easier.

Both facts remained available.

They left the cave wet and silent.

On the path home, Oren retold the passage as narrower than it had been. He made the water higher and his own return faster.

He did not mention taking Kalos’s wrist.

Kalos did not correct him.

## Conditional early surge

Wind outside may push water around the point sooner than expected.

Preparation changes the available responses:

- Mara’s conservative return time creates more margin.
- Neri’s shell markers provide visible evidence, even if one was placed imperfectly.
- Oren’s cord creates a safer handhold.
- Kalos’s injury or fatigue changes speed.
- Aro’s knowledge of the upper chamber may provide temporary height but not an exit.

The surge is not punishment for secrecy or reward for honesty. It is water arriving under particular conditions.

Any severe outcome must emerge from several causes, not from one morally coded choice.

# Closing interlude
## What Stayed

Use the opening and no more than two or three conditional passages in a given chronicle.

Rain continued through the following week.

The repaired roof seam held. Water gathered along the join, darkened the wood, and traveled toward the eave instead of falling into the house.

The visitors’ paddles stood among East Inlet’s paddles.

No one stumbled over them after the third day.

### If Seli’s journey became known

Seli began asking Veya about the upper channels in daylight, where Eda could hear.

Sometimes Eda answered the questions herself. Sometimes she found work at the other end of the house.

Aro showed Seli how the red inner fiber had been joined without smoke. Seli tried once and pulled the strand apart.

Aro laughed.

Seli tried again.

### If Pali’s gull story remained salient

Pali told the gull story differently each time.

In one version the bird was larger than the shed. In another, Kalos caught its leg but let go because it promised to return the fish skin. By the end of the week, Pali had been the one who saved Mara’s tray.

Kalos corrected the first two versions.

He let the third remain.

Neri set the pale marker shells above the work-shed door.

Oren said the arrangement had been his idea. Neri did not answer.

When the wind turned east, the shells clicked softly against the beam.

No one took them down.

The brace Oren tied across the cracked canoe rib remained through the next rain.

A visiting adult replaced it with a better one several days later. Before cutting Oren’s cord away, he showed two younger children how the temporary binding had carried the strain.

Oren was elsewhere.

### If Kalos took more oil

Eda measured the remaining oil before sealing it with the winter stores.

She paused over the amount.

Then she wrote no accusation into the room. She tightened the plug and handed the pouch to Veya.

Kalos watched from beside the hearth.

Aro did not look at him.

The visitors’ bundle remained dry.

Kalos’s cold passed after breakfast.

For several mornings afterward, he remembered the discomfort more readily than the thing it had protected.

Pali continued finding him in the night.

Aro and Seli developed a habit of speaking near the center of the house, where Kalos could hear their laughter but rarely the words before it.

## Memory interaction

> **When this part of the season returns later, what remains clearest?**

- Oil caught in the grain of a wooden bowl.
- The green light moving across the cave roof.
- Pali’s hand closed around his tunic in the dark.
- The shells clicking above the work-shed door.
- The grounded canoes and the mud holding everyone’s feet.

The selected image gains salience. It does not change what happened.

---

## Closing variants

### Oil

Years later, Kalos would forget who sat beside whom at the meal.

He remembered the bowl.

Ash had lightened the wood everywhere except the narrow places where the oil entered the grain.

### Green light

The cave became larger in memory.

The second chamber remained green even when he later knew the light had sometimes been gray, sometimes nearly colorless, and never still.

### Pali’s hand

He remembered Pali’s hand as smaller than it had been.

In memory, it closed around one fold of his tunic and stayed there through the entire night.

It had not.

### Shells

The shells remained above the shed until winter work covered the beam.

For several weeks, the wind could make them speak when none of the children were present.

### Low water

He remembered the mud holding his feet.

He did not remember how many people had helped pull him free.
